How to use

Pick a starting point (Orderly for talky pages, Action for slanted fast pages, Splash for big title pages), then move the sliders. Every change redraws the whole batch from the same seed. 🎲 New batch picks a new seed. Everything is made on your device from random numbers and set rules; nothing is uploaded.

The settings

  • Rows: how many rows of panels a page has. Only layouts with keeps only pages with that many panels.
  • Slanted rows / Slanted columns: how often and how much the borders lean. At 0 they are all straight.
  • Bleed: panels that run off the page edge (past the dotted inner frame) to the trim.
  • Split panels: panels cut again into two or three smaller ones. Big panels: one row or panel much bigger than the rest.
  • Reading: right to left like Japanese manga, or left to right like Western comics. Switching mirrors the same layouts; the numbers show the order.

Editing a layout

Click a page to open it large. Drag a border to move it; grab it near one end to tilt it instead. Click a panel to pick it (Shift for more) and press Delete to remove it; the panel next to it grows into the space. Ctrl+Z undoes. ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys go through the batch. Your edits stay with the batch while the settings stay the same.

Mutate

🧬 Mutate from this makes a batch of variations of one layout: the same structure with borders moved and tilted, panels split or merged and bleeds changed. Low stays close; High can add or remove rows. If a layout's rows (or columns) are all straight, they stay straight. Mutate a mutation to keep going.

Saving

Save SVG gives lines you can import as vectors; Save PNG gives a picture, 2400 px tall unless you change it. Background sets what's around and inside the panels: Clear leaves only the lines (a transparent picture), White or Black fill it. White inside the panels keeps the panels white with black or clear gutters. The numbers and the frame guide are left out of saved files unless you tick them. Copy link carries one layout, edits included.

In Clip Studio Paint

  • As a guide (recommended): open the PNG as a layer, make a frame border folder and trace the borders with the Divide frame tool. You get real masked panels.
  • As vectors: File › Import › Vector (Ver. 1.10 or later) brings in the SVG as lines, without frame folder masks.
  • As material: register layouts you like as materials and drag them in from the Material palette.
